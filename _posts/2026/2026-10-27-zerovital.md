[Gerovital H3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerovital) — the procaine hydrochloride formula developed in the 1950s by Ana Aslan and commercialized by Farmec in Romania — was marketed as a youth-restoring miracle, yet controlled clinical reviews and a U.S. National Institute on Aging evaluation found no reproducible evidence that it slows biological aging or extends lifespan. Procaine is rapidly hydrolyzed in the bloodstream into para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and diethylaminoethanol (DEAE), acting primarily as a short-lived local anesthetic and weak monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitor rather than addressing cellular senescence.

By contrast, downregulating the insulin and IGF-1 signaling pathway is one of the most conserved, reproducible longevity interventions in biology. Chronically elevated fasting insulin (hyperinsulinemia) over-activates mTOR and AKT while suppressing FOXO transcription factors and AMPK, locking cells in a pro-growth, pro-inflammatory state that inhibits autophagy — the cellular recycling mechanism discovered by Nobel laureate Yoshinori Ohsumi. Lowering fasting insulin through extended fasting intervals restores insulin sensitivity, triggers mitophagy and DNA repair, reduces visceral adiposity, and directly lowers the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, neurodegenerative decline, and cancer.

#### How does a normal person become obese?

The biology is simple. Every extra kilogram of body fat raises fasting blood insulin. As insulin climbs higher with every extra kilogram, eating every time you are hungry — or eating three meals a day — makes you gain more and more weight. The weight progression is logarithmic. Fasting insulin keeps rising without the person knowing what is happening inside their body… until a blood test reveals their fasting insulin level. Then they will convince themselves that insulin keeps the fat on their body no matter what they try: caloric deficit, exercise, diets, supplements, meds, eating less, eating healthier, building tons of muscle for no reason at all, and so on.

#### How do you reverse obesity as fast as it developed?

Since your fasting insulin is no longer that of a normal person, you can't keep eating like one. So instead of two or three meals a day, you adjust your eating schedule to your insulin level by eating every 24h, 36h, 48h or 72h. You need to switch between these intervals progressively as your hunger adjusts. If you eat every 24h, hunger will start creeping in around the 16th hour and move later and later until you feel hungry exactly at 24h. When you get hungry at exactly 24h, you switch to a 36h or 48h eating schedule. You repeat the process when you get hungry at 48h — or, if you can't do more than 48h, you keep eating at 48h intervals. For obese persons, eating at 72h intervals melts fat even faster, but it is not for everyone. Each kilogram lost this way also lowers your fasting insulin level, so fat burn gets faster and faster with every repeated 48h–72h eating interval.

#### How do you test insulin biology?

We can put the theory to the test. Say an obese person needs to eat 1600 calories per day in a caloric deficit, down from 3200 calories a day for maintenance. Suppose they eat 100 calories every hour, plus 8 hours of sleep. Over the course of a week, such a person would consume only half the calories they need. Would they lose or gain weight by the end of the week? Each 100-calorie dose would keep insulin levels elevated until the next hour, so they wouldn't lose any weight at all — in fact, they would gain weight depending on their existing body fat (which correlates with fasting insulin levels): those with less body fat would gain less, while those with more would gain more. If they ate all 1600 calories in a single sitting once a day, they would lose weight. If they split it into two meals of 800 calories, that would simply maintain their current weight.

The second test is so simple even AI gets it. Feed someone all the calories they need for a week on the first day, then fast for the next six days. In the first two days they will gain weight, because insulin is high and glucose needs to be pushed into cells. Over the next two days they will lose the weight gained in the first two. In the following two days they will lose even more than their starting weight, because insulin gets so low. So was there even a caloric deficit to begin with?

#### How do you optimize insulin spikes with diet?

No food is bad as long as you know when to eat it. Time matters more than diet alone. There are three types of foods, causing low, medium and huge insulin spikes:

Type 1: Vegetables — cooked or uncooked, in any combination with salt, spices, brine and vinegar. You can even enjoy them while hungry, inside your fasting window.

Type 2: Foods containing fiber, protein and healthy fats, combined into delicious meals. These are the foods that keep you healthy, don't cause weight gain and prevent diabetes.

Type 3: Sugar and white flour in highly processed foods, fruit juices and even homemade jams — if eaten in large portions. You don't need to avoid them completely; just enjoy them on special occasions like holidays, birthdays and events, or set a rhythm for them — say, enjoying your favorite ice cream once every two weeks.
